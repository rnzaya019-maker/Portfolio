01Where I started

The fastest way I learn a business is to build something real with its own data.

Understand the real thing first. Then make it easier to work with.

How I get up to speed. I go to the early, messy part of a project, before anything is written down cleanly. I talk to the people doing the work, look at whatever record already exists, and map what is actually happening. Only then does a tool or a process change make sense.

What I did here. I took ASI's own public product pages and turned them into a clean, interactive dataset, built end to end with a short, repeatable pipeline rather than assembled by hand. Everything you click below runs on that. It is a small working example of the kind of thing I would build on the job, pointed at a company I would like to join.

How it was built

  1. Collected public pagesProduct and catalogue pages for ASI and three peers, gathered with an open scraping tool. No logins, no personal data.
  2. Structured the contentNormalized into one row per catalogue page: line, model, any public price, specs.
  3. Computed the metricsCounts by line, category coverage, price visibility, and spec completeness, all reproducible from the saved data.
  4. Cross-checked against the live siteCategory-overview pages were separated from single products, and a cross-listed duplicate was removed, before anything was counted.

02The role, up close

Four responsibilities, and what each one would actually ask for at ASI.

The role John described blends four areas. Here is my read on what each one likely needs at a 28-person, in-house manufacturer like ASI, the day-to-day it involves, and where my background lines up. Grounded in what ASI publishes about itself, with the real scope to confirm together on the call.

Training Administration

Keep a growing, configurable product easy to learn.

ASI already runs a 24/7 self-serve support hub (manuals, how-to videos, live phone, Zoom, and FaceTime orientation) and already has a Training Manager. The equipment is technical and configurable, so the real need is upkeep: every configuration a customer buys should ship with material that is current, complete, and easy to find. Day to day that means matching content to the live product line, helping orientation run, and most likely supporting the existing Training function rather than replacing it, exact line to confirm.

What ASI already runs
  • 24/7 support hub: manuals, how-to videos
  • Live orientation by phone, Zoom, FaceTime
  • A Training Manager already on the team
Project Management

Hold the thread across a small, busy team.

At 28 people, ASI designs and builds in house, with engineering, marketing, service, and support all under one roof. A company this size rarely has a project office, so work tends to move through whoever can keep it moving. A new product, a process change, or a catalogue refresh all need one person keeping the steps, owners, and dates visible so nothing stalls between departments. I have run that kind of work end to end before, and I ran this page the same way, a checkpoint before each step.

The shop this runs inside
  • ~28 people, design and build in house
  • Machining, laser cutting, robotic bending
  • Engineering, marketing, service, support, one roof
Process & Procedure Development

Turn how it is done into how it is written down.

ASI is ISO 13485 certified and keeps quality control in house on purpose. A manufacturer like that runs on documented, repeatable procedure, and as it grows, the knowledge in people's heads has to become written steps a new hire can follow and an auditor can trust. The work is sitting with the people who do a task, writing down how it actually happens, and keeping conventions consistent as things change. This is the single strongest, most-repeated skill across every role I have held.

Why procedure matters here
  • ISO 13485 certified, documentation-driven
  • Quality control kept in house by design
  • Growth turns know-how into written SOPs
International Product Management

Make a manual export process repeatable.

ASI's own published buyers' terms show international orders handled case by case today: a quote by email or fax, freight, duties, and crating excluded and arranged separately, lead time simply described as longer. That works, but it does not scale cleanly. Growing international sales means turning quoting, logistics, and customs paperwork into a documented, repeatable flow. This is the piece I have done most directly, 23 international dealer clients at Autocom across Africa, the Caribbean, and the UK, and customs and import documentation at Kaneyama.

What ASI's buyers' terms show today
  • Quote intake: email or fax, case by case
  • Freight, duties, crating: excluded, arranged separately
  • Lead time: longer, not quantified publicly

05ASI and the field

A focused maker in a market of broad catalogues.

ASI and A-dec are focused equipment makers whose configured systems are mostly quote-based. Aseptico and DNTLworks are broad catalogues that also list parts and accessories. So these are compared within a shared category, never as one overall total, and price is shown only as visibility, never as amounts.

06The market around it

A growing market, and where ASI already sits in it.

The dental equipment market is growing, and ASI already plays in some of its fastest-moving corners. Strengths below come straight from the catalogue data on this page. The market figures are verified third-party research, cited in full, not estimated.

What stands out

  • All seven of ASI's product lines have public content, and every single-product page carries a full description and a photo end to end (…).
  • Where a feature list is published it runs deep: a median of … feature points and … words per page, enough to compare a configuration without calling for a quote.
  • The catalogue stays focused. Every one of ASI's 23 public pages is equipment it designs and builds, with no resold parts or refurbished stock mixed in (FIG.1).

Where the market is growing

  • The global dental equipment market is sized at $12.5B for 2025, projected to reach $20.9B by 2033, a 6.8% CAGR from 2026.1
  • The mobile and portable dental unit segment, exactly where ASI's Portable Dentistry and Delivery Systems & Carts lines sit, is projected to grow from $0.46B in 2026 to $0.90B by 2035, a 7.83% CAGR.2
  • 67% of rural U.S. areas are designated Dental Health Professional Shortage Areas, and rural residents are the least insured group measured (34%, against 29% urban and 24% suburban).3 A self-contained mobile unit is built to close exactly that gap, the story told in this page's hero film.

Worth a closer look

  • Most pricing is quote-based online: … of ASI's 23 pages show a price. Normal for configured equipment, and it means a self-serve buyer simply sees fewer numbers up front than on the broad catalogues.
  • Two lines, Portable Dentistry and Dental Assistant Systems, sit behind a single overview page each, so several configurations are named on the page but not yet detailed one page at a time.

What to keep an eye on

  • The broad catalogues (Aseptico, DNTLworks) publish pricing on most of their pages (…, FIG.2), lowering the research friction for a buyer who compares options online before requesting a quote.
  • All seven of ASI's public lines are physical delivery hardware. The faster-growing, software-adjacent corners of the market cited above (digital workflow, remote-care integration) do not appear in this public catalogue.

08If this kept going

How a one-off snapshot becomes something the team owns.

A one-off snapshot is a start. The value comes from keeping it current, trustworthy, and owned by the team.

A

Understand the real process first

Before automating anything, sit with the people who maintain the catalogue and learn how it actually gets updated today. The tool should fit that, not fight it.

B

Make the refresh repeatable

The whole pipeline here reruns from one command. Scheduled on a cadence, it would flag what changed since last time instead of being rebuilt by hand.

C

Keep a person in the loop

AI makes the gathering and first drafts fast. Judgement about what matters stays with the team, so their time goes to the decisions, not the busywork.

D

One source everyone trusts

Clear definitions and a single place to look, so a price or a spec means the same thing to everyone who relies on it.
